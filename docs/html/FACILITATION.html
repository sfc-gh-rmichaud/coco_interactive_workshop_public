Run of show (Eastern Time)

The session is 90 minutes: 20 presenting, 70 hands-on. Five labs at their full length would take about 135-145 minutes, so each lab runs as a checkpoint of its first steps; the rest is take-home. To make the checkpoints fit, Step 1 of Labs 21, 31 and 32 is a presenter demo of about 2 minutes (attendees watch, then run Steps 2-3), and their verification prompts ask only for the figure in "Done when". The per-step minutes are guidance from the lab READMEs, not measured in a live run; Lab 32 is the tightest block.

Blocks total 90 minutes. Polls run inside their blocks; they do not add time. There is no protected Q&A block: the co-host answers questions in chat throughout and surfaces the common ones to the presenter between labs. The catalog slides (15-16) are a ten-second signpost, not narration.

When running behind

The live schedule has almost no slack, and Labs 31 and 32 back to back are the heaviest stretch. Cut, in this order:

  1. The stretch prompts in Labs 10 and 11 (evidence table, defensibility check, audience rewrite).
  2. The Lab 32 follow-up "Where did the four-hour figure come from?" -- keep the Iberia check.
  3. Switch one of Lab 31 or Lab 32 to a presenter-led demo from a prepared account, and let attendees catch up on the other.

Never cut verification steps or the Lab 32 cleanup -- its Cortex Search service keeps running until it is dropped.

Polls

  1. Readiness (slide 8): *On a scale of 1-5, how ready are you to follow along hands-on?*
  2. Confidence check (during Lab 11): *How confident are you guiding CoCo through a multi-step data exploration?*
  3. Focus check (slide 12): *What is the biggest challenge you've encountered so far in the lab?*
  4. Closing: On a scale of 1-5, how helpful is this content for your role?

Readiness gates before the day

These are decisions and checks, not things the materials can do for you.

  • [ ] Account model decided. Either attendees bring an approved sandbox, or accounts are provisioned for them. If provisioned, logins and the COCO_WS_USER role exist and have been tested before the event.
  • [ ] Prework communicated. The registration follow-up sends attendees' administrators shared/00_admin_role_setup.sql (base grants only, down to STOP HERE), and tells attendees to pre-run the shared setup plus the Lab 11, 21, 31 and 32 setups. Without that prework, the 90-minute plan does not fit.
  • [ ] Attendee-role rehearsal. Run prework and every live block as COCO_WS_USER, not an admin role, in a disposable account. In particular, confirm that role can create the Lab 21 dynamic tables and the Lab 32 agent on its own schema. Time each checkpoint against the run of show.
  • [ ] Presenter fallback captured. A prepared account with Labs 31 and 32 already built, plus screenshots of each checkpoint, clearly labelled as prepared, for attendees who are blocked or when a lab becomes a demo.
  • [ ] Moderator confirmed.
  • [ ] Links final. Registration and on-demand recording URLs added to the README once they exist. Scan both QR codes on slide 1 with a phone.
  • [ ] Event copy approved by the event's legal and marketing review, including the change from two hours to 90 minutes.

On-demand version and follow-up

The event is converted to on-demand afterwards, so the materials assume a viewer working alone:

  • EVENT_GUIDE.md holds every prompt and "done when" criterion; nothing relies on something only said aloud.
  • Poll moments are marked in the run of show above; viewers can skip them.

Suggested follow-up email content: the repository link, EVENT_GUIDE.md, the full catalog, the take-home steps of today's five labs, the curated next steps (41, 61, 71), Lab 12 diagramming, and a reminder to run each lab's cleanup -- Lab 32 first.
